# 03 · Moonleaf Cozy Café — World & Characters

> Doc 03 of 09 · Status: Draft v0.1 · 2026-08-27 · Updated with Narrative Arc Integration
> Six characters, one room, one valley. Depth over breadth (P4).

## 1. The World: Hollowbrook Crossing

A small market settlement where three roads meet: the **human town** downriver, the **elven woods of Vessany** to the east, and a **mountain hold** (dwarven and giant-kin) up the switchbacks. Nobody's home is *here* — that's the point. Hollowbrook exists for people in between: couriers, pilgrims, traders, wanderers. The café is the only place anyone actually *stays* for a while.

**Tone guide:** gentle whimsy with quiet melancholy underneath. The world is safe, but its people carry weather — long journeys, old arguments, someone they miss. The café doesn't fix them; it warms them while they rest.

**Magic:** ambient and domestic only (see GDD §5). Leylines hum under the valley; moonleaf only glows on nights it rained that day; the hearth was lit forty years ago and never went out. Nobody studies any of this. It's just how mornings work here.

### Places referenced in dialogue/journal (never visited — one room game)
- **The Crossing itself:** a well-worn stone square, lantern posts, notice board (your daily tips source).
- **Vessany Woods:** bioluminescent in autumn; Sela's cart route passes its edge.
- **The Mountain Road:** closed by snow each winter; explains seasonal visitor changes.
- **Downriver:** "the city" — Fenwick's old life; never named, deliberately vague.

## 2. Tone Bible (for all writers)

- Dialogue is short. People speak in habits and preferences, not exposition.
- Warmth is shown through *specifics*: someone remembers your usual; a cup is refilled before being asked.
- Conflict exists but resolves within an arc, gently, without villains.
- Humor is dry and character-based (Bram's gruffness, Mops's judgment), never wacky.
- Never use: sarcasm aimed at the player, guilt-tripping, urgency language ("hurry!", "last chance!").
- Every character gets at least one moment of quiet grace per arc.

## 3. Player Character

**You**, the niece/nephew (player-chosen name + pronoun) of Aunt Marigold, who ran the café for forty years and recently passed. Her presence is felt through letters, her recipe notes in the margins of the journal, and the stories customers tell. She is the emotional spine of the meta-arc: *inheriting not just a shop, but a role in people's lives.* Her dialogue is written but never voiced; she exists in past tense and present tense simultaneously (her letters arrive weekly).

**Canonical Backstory (Doc 09 §2):**
- Visited summers as a child; learned to brew at Marigold's knee
- Marigold was the adult who *listened* — not the one who directed
- Last visit 7 years ago; life pulled them away (city, career, expectations)
- Correspondence dwindled to annual postcards, then silence
- **Inheritance:** temporary stewardship (14 days) → player chooses ending
- Marigold's lesson: "The café was never about coffee. It was about *witnessing* people."

## 5. Relationship & Scene Structure

- **Hearts 0→5 per character**, capped +1/day (doc 02 §5). Gain from favorite serves (+1.0), correct serves (+0.1), chats (+0.25), arc choices (+set amounts).
- Each character has **6 scenes**: intro (heart 0–1), three middles tied to their arc beats (2–3), resolution (4), epilogue (5). Scenes are 30–60 seconds, portrait + dialogue box, one choice offered in two middles (both choices are kind; they change flavor, not outcomes — P1).
- Travelers (generic sprites) exist to keep the room alive between regulars and provide coin flow; no arcs.

## 6. NPC Arc Integration with Narrative System (Doc 09)

### Thematic Mirroring

Each NPC arc resolves by reflecting the player's own journey:

| NPC | Arc Resolution | Player Reflection |
|-----|---------------|-------------------|
| **Fenwick** | Chooses to stay as letter-carrier | Player chooses what to carry forward |
| **Sela** | Stays "until the road clears" | Player decides what "staying" means |
| **Bram** | Rings the chime | Player hears the past and chooses the future |
| **Nia** | Builds a beautiful kettle, not a machine | Player chooses craft over efficiency |
| **Wren** | Reveals the usual | Player tastes their own childhood memory |
| **Mops** | Sleeps in new furniture | Player's worthiness anxiety soothed by presence |

### Arc Scene Structure (Per NPC)

| Scene | Purpose | Trigger | Branch Sensitivity |
|-------|---------|---------|-------------------|
| Intro (1) | Establish character, hook | Hearts ≥ 1, day ≥ 2 | None |
| Middle 1 (2) | Deepen, reveal vulnerability | Hearts ≥ 2, day ≥ 3 | Dialogue flavor (Category A) |
| Middle 2 (3) | Thematic conflict | Hearts ≥ 3, day ≥ 4 | Optional in low-CARE |
| Middle 3 (4) | Choice moment | Hearts ≥ 4, day ≥ 6 | Life-path branch (Category B) |
| Resolution (5) | Thematic payoff | Hearts ≥ 5, day ≥ 8 | Mandatory |
| Epilogue (6) | Post-arc gift/closure | Scene 5 complete | Gift varies by dimension |

### Category A — Flavor Choices (Scene Dialogue)

```typescript
// Example: Fenwick Scene 2 choice
choice: {
  prompt: "What do you say?",
  options: [
    { label: "That sounds heavy.", flavor: "Fenwick nods slowly. 'It is.'", hearts: +0.1 },
    { label: "You don't have to carry it alone.", flavor: "Fenwick smiles. 'Don't I?'", hearts: +0.25 },
    { label: "Tell me more about the route.", flavor: "Fenwick's eyes brighten. 'Well...'", hearts: +0.1 }
  ]
}
// ALL lead to same Scene 3. Only hearts + dialogue differ.
```

### Category B — Life-Path Branches (Behavior-Derived)

| Branch | Derived From | Changes |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| Letter sequence priority | Dimension thresholds | Which optional letters appear first |
| Scene availability | Hearts + dimension combos | Optional middle scenes (3–4) per NPC |
| Revelation ordering | Curiosity + Wren progress | Marigold Letter 3 vs. Basement discovery |
| NPC emphasis | Hearts distribution | Which NPC gets epilogue gift focus |
| Ending naturalness | Final dimension state | Which ending requires least "stretch" |

**Implementation Rule:** Category B branches are **never** presented as explicit choices. They emerge from the scheduler evaluating narrative state.

## 8. Seasonal Calendar (post-MVP framing)

8-night moon cycle + four seasons rotate window weather, décor recolors, and special orders (frostberries in summer, Root & Remedy in winter). MVP ships eternal late-autumn: misty, lamplit, pre-first-snow — the coziest possible default frame.

## 9. Writing Logistics

- All strings live in one localization file from day one (`strings.json` keyed like `fenwick.scene2.line3`) even though MVP is English-only.
- Voice/tone review checklist per scene: Does it pass the tone bible? Is anyone hurried or guilted? Is there one specific, physical detail?
- Line budgets: scene ≤ 20 lines · chat snippet ≤ 3 lines · letter ≤ 120 words.
